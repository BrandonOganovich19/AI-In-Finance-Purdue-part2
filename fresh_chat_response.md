**Prompt:** Is Costco a Buffett business? Give its ROE for the last five years.

## Response (as supplied)

Yes, Costco has the qualities Buffett looks for, and Berkshire owned it for two decades. The catch is the price, not the business.

The Berkshire history. Charlie Munger served on Costco’s board from January 1997, and Costco had first asked Buffett to join the board, but he declined and sent Munger instead. Berkshire held the stock from 2000 until June 2020, when the stake was about 4.3 million shares worth $1.3 billion and Buffett sold all of it. At the 2021 Berkshire meeting, Buffett said he had sold against Munger’s advice and that it was probably a mistake.

Why it fits. The business is simple, it has a durable moat in membership fees and scale, it carries little debt, and its returns on capital have been high for many years. A long-time Berkshire holding the company still praises meets Buffett’s test. The part that may not meet his test is valuation: on FY2026 EPS of $20.76, the stock has traded at roughly 50x earnings this year. Buffett usually wants a fair price as well as a great business.

The FY2022–2025 figures match published data, for example MarketScreener’s ROE series of 30.55%, 27.53%, 30.27% and 30.69%. I calculated FY2026 from the year-end release: net income of $9.226 billion, up from $8.099 billion, and total equity of $35,803 million versus $29,164 million.

Some context for the numbers:

FY2024 is slightly inflated. The $15-per-share special dividend in January 2024 reduced equity, and lower equity raises ROE.
The returns don’t come from debt. Costco’s leverage is mostly supplier payables, because it sells inventory before it has to pay for it. That float-like feature is something Buffett values.
The method changes the figure. Using ending equity instead of average equity gives about 2–3 points lower in most years, for example FY2025 at 27.77%.
