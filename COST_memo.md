# COST — Own the business

*AI-simulated Buffett perspective for a learning exercise; not a statement by Warren Buffett. Business quality only.*

**Disclaimer:** This project was made with Codex. I am not a financial advisor. For educational purposes only; this is not investment advice.

I want a business whose earnings turn into cash an owner can use. Costco's reported results give me a solid starting point: revenue rose from **226,954** to **303,154** and net income from **5,844** to **9,226**, all in USD millions. The lab's owner-earnings proxy rose from **3,501** to **9,382** and remained positive throughout the displayed history.

Returns on year-end equity stayed above the **15.0%** hurdle. The latest **25.8%** is encouraging, but I would not confuse a high accounting return with proof of a durable moat. Owner earnings also slipped from **6,745** to **6,629** before recovering: good businesses do not move in a straight line.

My verdict is **own the business**, conditional on further qualitative diligence. The cash record supports an inference of productive economics; it does not establish customer loyalty, management integrity, or the durability of cost advantages. I would examine those claims before committing capital. Total capital spending includes expansion as well as maintenance, and working-capital movements can lift or depress operating cash flow. This simplified proxy cannot settle normalized earning power. Deteriorating cash conversion or evidence that attractive returns depend on an unusually small equity base would make me reconsider. Without a stock price or valuation, I make no claim about whether the shares are a bargain.

**Brandon's decision:** I provisionally trust the business verdict, but it does not establish a moat or an attractive stock price.
